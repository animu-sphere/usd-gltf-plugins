# 0016: Index of refraction

Decision record, 2026-11-03. It extends the networks of the
[factor-only decision](../architecture/MATERIAL_FACTORS.md). [MATERIALS](../design/MATERIALS.md#index-of-refraction)
specifies the result, and the [capability matrix](../reference/CAPABILITY_MATRIX.md) records current
evidence.

## Context

`KHR_materials_ior` sets `ior`, 1.5 by default, which in the core metallic-roughness material changes
only the Fresnel term of its dielectric part: its F0 becomes `((ior - 1) / (ior + 1))^2`, 0.04 at the
default, and its F90 stays 1. A metal's Fresnel term comes from its base color and does not change.
Valid values are 1 and above, and 0, which makes the Fresnel term 1 at every angle and lets a renderer
treat the ior as a very large finite value. The extension has no texture, and glTF forbids it on an
unlit material. Transmission and volume extensions also read it, but they are not implemented.

Both shaders already have an `ior` input of default 1.5, read in the same way:

- MaterialX `gltf_pbr` (1.39.5, the version OpenUSD 26.08 builds) computes its dielectric F0 as
  `((1 - ior) / (1 + ior))^2`, multiplied by `specular_color` and `specular`, with F90 equal to
  `specular`, which are 1 by default. It declares `ior` uniform. Its transmission also refracts by it,
  through `1 / ior`.
- UsdPreviewSurface's specification uses `ior` for the Fresnel term of objects with specular
  components. Storm's `previewSurface.glslfx` for OpenUSD 26.08 computes, in the metallic workflow,
  F0 as `R^2 * specColor` mixed toward `specColor` by `metallic`, where `R = (1 - ior) / (1 + ior)` and
  `specColor` is 1 for a dielectric, with F90 equal to `specColor`. The diffuse term is scaled by
  1 minus the Fresnel term.

At an ior of 0, both formulas give an F0 of 1, but MaterialX would refract by `1 / 0`, which is
infinite, in a transmission term whose weight is 0. Its product with 0 could still be NaN. The
UsdPreviewSurface specification does not say what an ior of 0 means.

## Options

- **Ignore the extension, with a warning,** which shows the default F0 of 0.04.
- **Bake the ior into `specular`,** scaling F0 by its ratio to 0.04. That changes F90 too, so it is not
  the same Fresnel term, and an F0 above 1 needs `specular_color` as well.
- **Author `ior` in both shaders,** whose formula is glTF's.

## Decision

Author `ior` in both shaders:

| glTF `ior` | `preview` (UsdPreviewSurface) `ior` | `mtlx` (`gltf_pbr`) `ior` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5, or absent | Not authored | Not authored |
| 1 and above | The value | The value, uniform |
| 0 | 1000000 | 1000000, uniform |
| Below 1, other than 0 | The import fails | The import fails |

An ior of 0 is authored as 1000000, the finite value glTF allows, whose F0 is 0.999996 and whose
`1 / ior` is finite. A value of the extension that is not an object, an `ior` that is not a number,
and one too large for a float fail, with a diagnostic naming the extension; an ior between 0 and 1
fails for the material that uses it. An unlit material authors no ior, because it reflects nothing, and
warns if it has one other than 1.5; its value is still checked, as are its other fallback properties.

## Evidence and limits

- Tests assert both networks for each kind of value, required and optional, unlit, and each failure.
  The sample check compares the ior of each sample material.
- The [display check](../reference/CAPABILITY_MATRIX.md#display-checks) renders a black dielectric
  quad under a light at iors of 1, 1.5, 2 and 0, and a white metal for comparison.
- The equivalence with glTF is read from the shaders' sources, not displayed. Storm's
  UsdPreviewSurface mixes a partly metallic F0 differently from glTF, at any ior, as it did before.
- `gltf_pbr` multiplies the reflection over its diffuse base by directional albedo, so at a rough ior
  of 0 a little of the base color shows, where glTF's Fresnel term of 1 hides it.
- Other renderers may read UsdPreviewSurface's `ior` only for refraction, or not at all.
